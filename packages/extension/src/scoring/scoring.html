How Review Points are counted

The badge shows ↑ RP, the Review Points you gave by reviewing, and ↓ RP, the Review Points you asked for with your own PRs in review. Both sides use the same unit. This page is the whole formula: the extension computes your badge in this browser with exactly these weights, and nothing leaves it except GitHub API calls made with your token.

Window

A rolling -day window (h) on both sides: the reviews you submitted, and your PRs marked ready for review. Each record then decays with its age.

Weighted lines

Every count of lines is weighted by its file class, on both sides, so ↑ and ↓ stay in the same unit: 

weighted lines = Σ classes  class weight × lines of that class
Class Weight

↑ RP, per review you submit

↑ review = ( covered × action weight
           + commented × comment multiplier
           + viewed × viewed bonus )
         × decay(age of the review)
         × rescue
  1. covered: the weighted lines changed since the commit of your previous review on the PR, or the full diff on your first review.
  2. action weight: set by the kind of review.

  3. commented: the weighted lines of your review comments' ranges (start_line..line), at most lines per comment, times the comment multiplier of . A thread reply without a review earns this term only.
  4. viewed: the weighted lines of the files you marked Viewed, times the viewed bonus of .
  5. rescue: × when the PR had waited more than h with no review when you reviewed it. Otherwise the term changes nothing.

↓ RP, per PR you own

↓ PR = size tiers(weighted lines of the PR) × decay(time since ready for review)

The size tiers apply to marginal lines, so there is no cliff at a tier boundary. They apply to ↓ only: on ↑ they would make large PRs attractive to reviewers.

Weighted lines Each line counts

The clock starts when the PR is marked ready for review, so draft time does not count. A review received, a merge or a close does not reset it: the PR counts until the end of the window.

Decay

Full weight for the first h, then linear down to at the end of the window, then nothing. A record dated up to h in the future counts as brand new: clocks drift. Further in the future, it counts for nothing.

Rounding

  • Times round to the nearest hour.
  • Line counts round to the nearest . Commented lines round to a multiple of without bias, in a direction drawn from a hash of the review, so a one-line comment keeps its expected value and the badge does not flicker.
  • At most the newest reviews and PRs count.
  • ↑ and ↓ round to whole points.
  • The ratio is ↑ / ↓, rounded to decimals. When ↓ is zero there is no ratio: the badge shows ∞ when ↑ is above zero, and — when both are zero.

With these weights, a plain approval of a PR under weighted lines balances one PR of the same size of your own, at the same age, at exactly ratio one. Comments, Viewed files and rescues push the ratio up; large PRs of your own pull it down.
